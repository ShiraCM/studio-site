Expanded living space.

The family wanted more room to gather, a proper place for laundry and better storage in the primary suite. Working from the developer’s plan, we weighed what they could give up for the space they would use most.

The plans

A bedroom given back to the living room.

The developer’s plan placed a bedroom between the living area and the rest of the apartment. The family chose one generous shared space instead, so its walls came down, and the bathrooms and the primary suite were redrawn around the change.

The changes

The decisions, one by one.

More room to gather

The living and dining area felt tight. Opening the adjoining bedroom into it gave the family one generous shared space.

A room for laundry

The washer stood in the main bathroom. The guest toilet, with a strip borrowed from the main bathroom, became a dedicated laundry room.

Storage that earns its space

In the developer’s primary suite the door opened straight onto the beds, and a walk-in closet spent floor area on circulation. We turned the beds and replaced the walk-in with built-in closets that hold more.

A larger en-suite

The original en-suite was very tight. Reworking the closets around it made room for a larger bathroom.

Close-up

The laundry gets a room of its own.

The same corner of the apartment, before and after: the guest toilet becomes the laundry room, and the washer leaves the main bathroom.

Custom closet

A closet for the primary bedroom, found next door.

The second bedroom, which was the developer’s primary bedroom, gave up a strip along its wall. A closet now fills that strip and opens into the new primary bedroom, beside the way into the en-suite.

The bedroom it came from keeps room for its two beds, and the closet runs from wall to wall at full hanging depth.
