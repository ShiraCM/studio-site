Reimagining a developer blueprint for how a family actually lives.

Starting from a builder’s floor plan, we reallocated underused square footage to prioritize the spaces this family uses every single day: an expansive living area built for hosting, a proper laundry room, and smarter storage in the primary suite. Designed with aging in place in mind, every room was planned with generous clearances and effortless accessibility—creating a home that feels open today and adapts seamlessly for years to come.

The plans

Sacrificing a bedroom for a larger common room.

The original layout wedged a bedroom between the living area and the rest of the apartment. We took down its walls to make one open space for gathering. Then we redrew the bathrooms and primary suite around how the family lives: the guest toilet became a laundry room, and the primary bedroom gained a wall of closets and a larger en-suite.

The design process

The case for giving up a bedroom.

When this couple approached us after purchasing their apartment, they believed they had bought a four-bedroom home with generous living space. To test what the floor plan could actually support, we modeled five distinct layouts in 3D using true-to-scale furniture. Options 1 and 2 felt far too cramped overall. Option 3 was a step forward, though the kitchen still bled into the living area. Option 4 finally unlocked a comfortable living room, but at the expense of a cramped kitchen. Seeing those trade-offs clearly led to Option 5: intentionally giving up a bedroom to deliver the spacious living area and proper, full-sized kitchen the clients had envisioned all along.

Signature Detail

A compact bedroom, with room to sleep and to store.

In the developer’s plan, this was the primary bedroom. We gave 24 inches of it to the new primary bedroom for its closet, and kept everything this room needs.

The original developer layout left dead space to the left of the entry. By shifting the door as far left as possible—leaving just enough clearance for a deep wall-to-wall closet—we unlocked the right side of the room to comfortably house two beds and a newly carved-out closet niche for the adjacent primary bedroom.
